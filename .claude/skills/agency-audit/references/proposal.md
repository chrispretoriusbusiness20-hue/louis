# Proposal construction — template, tiers, ROI arithmetic

Read this before writing a proposal. The rule that governs everything here: **finding → consequence → service → price.** Every line item traces to a numbered audit finding. If it doesn't, cut it.

## Prerequisites

A proposal needs an audit behind it. If none exists in the conversation, run Stage 2 first. You also need two client numbers for any ROI math: **monthly traffic (or lead volume)** and **average ticket (or customer value)**. If you don't have them, ask for exactly those two figures — do not invent them.

## Tier construction

Three tiers, always. Each tier is built from audit findings, not from a service menu.

### Entry — "the yes"
- **Contents:** the audit's quick wins, nothing else. Everything deliverable inside two weeks.
- **Pricing:** fixed price, low enough to clear without a committee. This tier's job is to be easy to say yes to and to prove competence fast.
- **Trap to avoid:** don't pad it. An Entry tier with strategy sessions in it stops being low-risk.

### Core — "the one you want"
- **Contents:** everything that addresses the critical findings. Includes Entry (say so explicitly — "everything in Entry, plus").
- **Pricing:** the obvious middle. Typically 3–5× Entry. Fixed price or short engagement (6–10 weeks).
- **Trap to avoid:** don't let Core sprawl into Full. If it fixes things the audit didn't flag as critical, move them up a tier or cut them.

### Full — "the anchor"
- **Contents:** Core plus the single Opportunity from the audit, delivered as a retainer or phased engagement.
- **Pricing:** priced so Core looks reasonable — typically 2–3× Core on an annualized basis. Some clients take it; that's a bonus, not the plan.
- **Trap to avoid:** Full must still trace to findings. It's the opportunity operationalized, not "everything we sell."

## ROI arithmetic

The format is always: **assumption → arithmetic → range.** Never a bare number, never a promise.

Template:

> If [the fix] moves [metric] by [conservative delta] on your [client's stated volume] at your [client's stated ticket], that's roughly [low]–[high] per year against [price] of work.

Worked example:

> If the 4.2 → 4.6 rating lift moves conversion 1pt on ~300 monthly visits at your ~$180 ticket, that's ~$6.5k/yr against $2.4k of work.
> (Arithmetic: 300 visits × 1% × $180 × 12 months = $6,480.)

Rules:
- Use conservative deltas: 0.5–2pt conversion moves, not 10pt fantasies.
- Show the arithmetic inline or in a footnote so the client can rerun it with their own numbers.
- Give a range when inputs are soft; the honesty costs nothing and survives month three.
- One ROI projection per tier is enough. Stacked projections read as sales math.

## Proposal template

```
# Proposal — [Business name]
Prepared for [decision-maker name], [date]

## What we found
Three to five numbered findings, lifted from the audit. Each one line:
what's wrong · what it costs them · how we know. Numbered — the tiers
cite these numbers.

## What we'd do

### Option 1 — [Entry name] · $X, fixed
Fixes findings [#, #]. Delivered in [timeframe].
- [Line item → finding #]
- [Line item → finding #]

### Option 2 — [Core name] · $X, fixed  ← recommended
Everything in Option 1, plus fixes findings [#, #, #]. Delivered in [timeframe].
- [Line item → finding #]
- [Line item → finding #]
ROI sketch: [assumption → arithmetic → range]

### Option 3 — [Full name] · $X/mo, [term]
Everything in Option 2, plus [the opportunity]. Ongoing.
- [Line item → finding # / opportunity]

## What happens next
One step. Name it. ("Reply yes to Option 2 and we start Monday" —
not a menu of ways to maybe get in touch.)

## Assumptions & exclusions
Access you need, content they must supply, what's explicitly out of
scope. Short. This section prevents month-two scope arguments.
```

## Style rules

- Mark Core as recommended, once. Don't oversell it — the tier structure does the selling.
- Name tiers after outcomes ("Get found locally"), not deliverables ("SEO package").
- No jargon the decision-maker wouldn't use. If the audit said "no meta description," the proposal says "your homepage is invisible in search results."
- Keep the whole proposal under a page and a half. The audit is the appendix, not the body.
- Never contradict the audit. If the audit scored Compliance 85, don't sell a compliance overhaul.
